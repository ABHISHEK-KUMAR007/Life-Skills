# 1. Tiny Habits - BJ Fogg

### Question 1

**In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?**

The most interesting idea for me was that we don't need a lot of motivation to build a habit. We can start with a very small action. For example, instead of studying for two hours, I can start with just 5 minutes. Once I start, it becomes easier to continue.

# 2. Tiny Habits - Core Message

### Question 2

**How can you use B = MAP to make new habits easier? What are M, A and P?**

B = MAP means:

- **M – Motivation:** How much I want to do the behavior.
- **A – Ability:** How easy or difficult the behavior is.
- **P – Prompt:** A reminder or trigger that tells me to do it.

I can make a new habit easier by keeping the action small, having a clear reminder, and connecting it with something I already do.

### Question 3

**Why is it important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of a habit?**

Celebrating after completing a habit gives me a positive feeling and makes me want to repeat the behavior. Even a small celebration like saying "Good job" to myself can make the habit feel rewarding. I liked the idea that we should celebrate the small progress instead of waiting for a big result.

### Question 4

The most interesting idea for me was getting **1% better every day**. A small improvement may not look important today, but doing it consistently can create a big difference over time. I can apply this to coding by learning one concept or solving one problem every day.

# 4. Atomic Habits

### Question 5

**What is the book's perspective about Identity?**

Atomic Habits explains that habits help shape our identity. Instead of only thinking about what I want to achieve, I should think about the person I want to become.

For example, instead of saying, "I want to solve 100 coding problems," I can think, **"I am a person who practices coding regularly."**

### Question 6

**How can you make a habit easier to do?**

I can make a habit easier by reducing the effort required to start it. I can prepare everything beforehand and start with a very small version of the habit.

For example, instead of planning to study for two hours, I can start by studying for 10–15 minutes.

### Question 7

**How can you make a habit harder to do?**

For a bad habit, I can make it harder by adding extra steps between me and the habit.

For example, if I want to reduce social media use, I can keep my phone away from my study table, log out of the apps, or remove unnecessary apps.

# 5. Reflection

### Question 8

**Pick one habit that you would like to do more of.**

I would like to do **coding practice more regularly**.

- **Make the cue obvious:** Keep my laptop and coding material ready.
- **Make it attractive:** Practice topics or problems that I find interesting.
- **Make it easy:** Start with one problem or 15 minutes of coding.
- **Make it satisfying:** Mark the problem as completed and appreciate the progress.

My main goal is to become consistent rather than trying to do too much in one day.

### Question 9

**Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of.**

I would like to reduce **unnecessary phone and social media scrolling**.

- **Make the cue invisible:** Keep my phone away while studying.
- **Make it unattractive:** Remind myself that unnecessary scrolling wastes my time.
- **Make it difficult:** Log out of social media or remove the apps.
- **Make it unsatisfying:** When I notice myself scrolling without a reason, I will stop and do something more useful.

I don't want to completely stop using my phone. I just want to use it more consciously and avoid using it automatically.
