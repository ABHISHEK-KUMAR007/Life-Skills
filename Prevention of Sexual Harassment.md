# Understanding and Addressing Sexual Harassment

**Sexual harassment** refers to unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or other verbal, non-verbal, or physical behavior of a sexual nature that creates an uncomfortable, hostile, intimidating, or offensive environment.

---

## Categories of Sexual Harassment

Sexual harassment generally falls into two main categories:

### 1. Quid Pro Quo ("This for That")

This occurs when someone demands or expects sexual favors in return for career or academic benefits, such as promotions, salary increases, favorable treatment, or job security.

### 2. Hostile Work Environment

This involves behavior that is severe or persistent enough to create an intimidating, hostile, humiliating, or uncomfortable atmosphere.

#### Examples of Verbal & Non-Verbal Behaviors:

* **Unwelcome Remarks:** Sexual comments, inappropriate jokes, stories, or conversations.
* **Persistent Advances:** Repeatedly asking someone out even after they have clearly declined.
* **Inappropriate Comments:** Making unwanted remarks about someone's body, clothing, appearance, or physical features.
* **Intrusive Inquiries:** Asking personal or explicit questions about someone's private life or sexual experiences.
* **Gossip:** Spreading rumors, speculation, or gossip of a sexual nature about another person.
* **Explicit Displays:** Displaying sexually explicit posters, images, videos, or screensavers in shared or professional spaces.
* **Digital Harassment:** Sending unwanted sexually suggestive emails, messages, emojis, images, or other media.

---

## What to Do: Key Steps to Take

If you experience or witness sexual harassment, consider taking the following steps:

### 1. Address the Conduct (If Safe)

If you feel comfortable and safe doing so, clearly tell the individual that their behavior is unwelcome, inappropriate, and needs to stop immediately. Setting a firm and explicit boundary can sometimes prevent the behavior from continuing.

### 2. Document Everything

Maintain a detailed and private record of each incident. Include:

* Dates, exact times, and locations.
* What was said or done, as accurately as possible.
* Names of anyone who witnessed the incident.
* Copies or screenshots of relevant text messages, emails, or chat conversations.

### 3. Report Internally

If appropriate, submit a formal complaint through the established channels within the organization:

* **Workplace:** Contact HR, an Internal Complaints Committee (such as the ICC / POSH Committee in India), or a direct supervisor.
* **Educational Institutions:** Contact the Internal Committee, Title IX coordinator, or campus safety office.

### 4. Seek External Support & Helplines

If necessary, reach out to support services, counseling resources, or legal professionals for additional assistance:

* **Emergency / Police:** Contact local emergency services (e.g., **112** / **100** in India).
* **National Helplines:** Women's Helplines (e.g., **1091** in India).
